## Work more effectively when Confluence and Jira live side by side

Confluence gives your Jira work a home for plans, decisions, documentation, and shared context. If you already track work in Jira, Confluence helps your team capture the thinking behind that work and collaborate on it in real time.

You do not need to learn a whole new workflow to get started. Start by exploring how Jira content appears in Confluence, then create a few pages and invite teammates to build shared knowledge together.

<Callout kind="tip">

Confluence extends Jira by giving your team a shared space for knowledge, planning, and collaboration. Use Jira to track work, and use Confluence to organize the context, decisions, and documentation around that work.

</Callout>

## Confluence and Jira together

Confluence and Jira connect your team's work and knowledge in one flow. You can display Jira work directly in Confluence pages and move between both tools without losing context.

- **Display Jira work in Confluence** — Embed Jira links directly into Confluence pages. Preview status changes and work item details without leaving Confluence.
- **Move between Jira and Confluence** — When you link a Jira work item to a Confluence page, the Jira work items field appears in the page details panel for quick navigation.

<Callout kind="info">

Try these first tasks:

- Link a Jira issue to a Confluence page
- Embed a Jira filter on a Confluence page
- Use the page details panel to navigate between linked Jira items

</Callout>

## Confluence 101

Spaces are where teams organize ideas, projects, and documentation in Confluence. Think of a space as a home for a team or initiative, with content grouped so people can find what they need quickly.

As your content grows, spaces help you keep work structured instead of scattered across separate pages. That makes it easier to browse, maintain, and share knowledge across the team.

<Callout kind="info">

Spaces can be organized with folders, and you can drag and drop content to reorder it or nest it under other content.

</Callout>

## What's next

Your first few actions in Confluence should be small and practical. Explore how your team already uses spaces, create one piece of content, and invite someone else in so collaboration becomes part of the habit from the start.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Explore a Space" icon="compass">

Look around an existing space to see how other teams organize their work. Pay attention to how pages are grouped, named, and used for ongoing projects.

A good first success signal is finding a space where you can clearly identify active work, reference material, and team knowledge in separate areas.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a Page" icon="plus">

Use the **Create** button to start your first page or live doc. Begin with something useful and small, such as meeting notes, a project outline, or a shared checklist.

You are on the right track when your page is visible in the space and ready for others to edit or comment on.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Invite a Teammate" icon="user-plus">

Bring someone into your space so you can collaborate in real time. Shared editing, comments, and mentions make Confluence more useful as soon as more than one person contributes.

Success looks like a teammate joining the page, adding feedback, or editing alongside you live.

  </Step>
</Steps>
